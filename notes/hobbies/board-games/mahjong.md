# Mahjong

Hong Kong Old Style, the most commonly played English-language ruleset. Riichi (Japanese) and American mahjong differ enough that they need their own notes.

## Goal

Be the first to complete a hand of 4 sets and 1 pair that is worth at least the table minimum in faan, then collect from the player who fed you the tile — or from everyone if you drew it yourself.

## Limitations

| Limit               | Value                         | Notes                                                                 |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chow                | From the player on your left  | Pung and kong may be claimed from anyone                              |
| Concealed hand      | 13 tiles, 14 mid-turn         | Exposed melds sit on the table and can no longer be changed           |
| Flowers and seasons | 8 bonus tiles                 | Never part of the hand; replaced from the wall the moment they appear |
| Game length         | 16 hands minimum              | 4 rounds of 4, longer whenever the dealer repeats                     |
| Hand shape          | 4 sets + 1 pair               | Special hands such as Thirteen Orphans are the exception              |
| Minimum to win      | 3 faan                        | A table rule; casual tables drop it to 1 or 0                         |
| Players             | 4                             | Seated East, South, West, North counter-clockwise                     |
| Scoring cap         | 13 faan                       | Tables often cap lower, at 8 or 10                                    |
| Tiles               | 144                           | 136 playing tiles + 8 bonus tiles                                     |
| Wall                | 36 tiles each, 18 stacks of 2 | Pushed together into a square                                         |

## Setup

- Roll dice to set seats and decide who starts as East, the dealer. The other seats run South, West, North counter-clockwise.
- Each player shuffles and builds a wall of 36 tiles, 18 stacks 2 high, and pushes it into a square.
- East rolls to choose which wall is broken and where.
- Deal 4 tiles at a time, counter-clockwise from the break, until everyone holds 12; then 1 more each. East takes an extra tile and starts with 14.
- Everyone reveals any flowers and seasons and draws replacements from the far end of the wall, repeating until no one holds a bonus tile.
- East discards first.

## On Your Turn

A turn is draw, optionally meld, then discard — always ending with a discard.

1. Draw
2. Meld
3. Discard

### 1. Draw

- Take the next tile from the live wall, or take the previous player's discard by claiming it.
- Drawing a flower or season means setting it aside face up and drawing a replacement from the far end of the wall.

### 2. Meld

- Optional. Expose a chow, pung or kong face up in front of you.
- A kong leaves you a tile short, so draw a replacement from the far end of the wall.
- An exposed meld is locked — this is the cost of speed, since concealed hands score more.

### 3. Discard

- Place 1 tile face up in the middle. Your hand returns to 13 tiles.
- You may not discard the tile you just claimed.

### Claiming a Discard

Any player may interrupt the turn order to claim a discard:

| Claim   | Who can claim                | Effect                                     |
| ------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Chow    | The player to its right only | Completes a run; play resumes from you     |
| Kong    | Anyone                       | Completes a four; draw a replacement tile  |
| Mahjong | Anyone                       | Completes the winning hand                 |
| Pung    | Anyone                       | Completes a triplet; play resumes from you |

- Priority: mahjong beats pung and kong, which beat chow.
- A claim skips everyone between the discarder and the claimer.

## Tiles

| Group               | Tiles                                   | Count |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Bamboo              | 1–9, 4 copies each                      | 36    |
| Characters          | 1–9, 4 copies each                      | 36    |
| Dots                | 1–9, 4 copies each                      | 36    |
| Dragons             | Red, Green, White, 4 copies each        | 12    |
| Flowers and seasons | 4 flowers + 4 seasons, 1 copy each      | 8     |
| Winds               | East, South, West, North, 4 copies each | 16    |

Terminals are the 1s and 9s; honours are the winds and dragons.

## Sets

| Set  | Shape                                |
| ---- | ------------------------------------ |
| Chow | 3 consecutive tiles in one suit      |
| Kong | 4 identical tiles                    |
| Pair | 2 identical tiles, the hand's "eyes" |
| Pung | 3 identical tiles                    |

Honours cannot form chows.

## Flowers and Seasons

- They never count as part of your 13 tiles — reveal and replace them immediately.
- A flower or season matching your seat wind is worth 1 faan, which counts toward the minimum.
- Many casual tables remove all 8 to speed the game up.

## Game End

A hand ends when either happens:

- A player declares mahjong with 4 sets and a pair worth at least the minimum faan.
- The live wall runs out with no winner — a washout. Nobody pays.

Then:

- The dealer repeats if the dealer won or the hand washed out.
- Otherwise the deal passes counter-clockwise.
- Once the deal has gone all the way around, the prevailing wind advances: East, South, West, North. Four rounds is a full game, so 16 hands minimum.

## Scoring

Faan from every condition the hand meets are added together, then converted to points:

| Faan | Points |
| ---- | ------ |
| 3    | 8      |
| 4    | 16     |
| 5    | 24     |
| 6    | 32     |
| 7    | 48     |
| 8    | 64     |
| 9    | 96     |
| 10+  | 128    |

Payment:

- **Won on a discard** — the discarder pays the full value.
- **Self-drawn** — all 3 opponents pay the full value each.

Common faan sources:

| Source                                   | Faan   |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------ |
| All chows, no honours                    | 1      |
| All pungs                                | 3      |
| Concealed hand                           | 1      |
| Dragon pung                              | 1 each |
| Full flush, one suit and nothing else    | 7      |
| Great Dragons, all 3 dragon pungs        | 8      |
| Half flush, one suit plus honours        | 3      |
| Pung of your seat wind or the round wind | 1 each |
| Self-draw                                | 1      |
| Thirteen Orphans                         | 13     |

## Table Rules

Settle these before the first deal — they differ table to table:

- The faan minimum, usually 3, sometimes 1 or 0.
- The faan cap, 13 by the book but often 8 or 10.
- Whether points double every faan ("full spicy") or every other faan above 4 ("half spicy", the table above).
- Whether flowers and seasons are in play at all.
- Whether the dealer's repeat is worth extra.

## Quick Tips

- Decide early whether you are going for speed or value — the 3 faan minimum punishes hands that meld everything cheaply.
- A half flush is the most reachable way to clear the minimum from a bad start.
- Honours are safe to hold early and dangerous to discard late, since a pung of them may be all an opponent needs.
- Watch what each player is not discarding; that suit is the one they are collecting.
