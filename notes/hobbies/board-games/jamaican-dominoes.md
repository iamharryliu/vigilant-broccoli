## Goal

Be the first player to play out all your bones, or hold the lowest pip count when the layout blocks. A team wins the match by taking 6 games before the other side takes any — "six love".

## Limitations

| Limit        | Value                     | Notes                                                                         |
| ------------ | ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bones dealt  | 7 per player              | All 28 bones are dealt in partner play                                        |
| Boneyard     | None                      | Nothing to draw from, so you either play or pass                              |
| Doubles      | Played crosswise          | Layout convention only; a double still matches on its single number           |
| Layout ends  | 2                         | Only the two ends of the line are ever playable                               |
| Match length | 6 games                   | 6–0 is a "six love"                                                           |
| Passing      | Only when you cannot play | You may never pass while holding a playable bone                              |
| Players      | 4 in 2 fixed partnerships | Partners sit opposite each other; see [Cut Throat](#cut-throat) for solo play |
| Set          | Double six, 28 bones      | Pip values 0–6, highest bone is 6-6                                           |

## Setup

- 4 players at a square table, partners facing each other.
- Wash the bones face down and each player takes 7. There is no boneyard — every bone is in someone's hand.
- First game: the player holding the double six poses it.
- Later games: the team that won the previous game decides which of them poses, and that player may pose any bone.
- After a tied game: the double six holder poses again.

Play passes counter-clockwise.

## On Your Turn

Do exactly 1 of:

1. Play a bone
2. Pass

### 1. Play a Bone

- Match one end of your bone to the number at either open end of the layout.
- Doubles are laid crosswise but still only open the one number.

### 2. Pass

- Only legal when no bone in your hand matches either end.
- Announce it — knocking the table is the usual signal — and play moves on.

## The Layout

- The line grows from the posed bone in both directions, leaving exactly 2 open ends.
- A number that already appears 7 times across the layout and hands is exhausted; the one remaining bone carrying it makes a "hard end" that only its holder can answer.
- Counting what has been played and what your partner passed on is most of the game.

## Game End

A game ends the moment either happens:

- **Domino** — a player plays their last bone. Their team wins the game.
- **Block** — every player passes in turn with bones still in hand. Each team totals the pips left in both partners' hands; the lower total wins.

Equal totals on a block is a tie: no points, and the double six poses the next game.

## Scoring

- Winning a game scores 1 point.
- Winning on the key bone scores 2 points.
- The first team to 6 points wins the match.
- When the other team wins a game, the score resets to 0–0 — so a "six love" is 6 games taken without reply.
- A tied game scores nothing and leaves the score where it stands.

## Cut Throat

Every player for themselves, 2 to 4 players:

| Players | Bones each | Notes                                                       |
| ------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2       | 14         | Some tables deal 7 and leave the rest as a boneyard to draw |
| 3       | 9          | The double blank is removed from the set                    |
| 4       | 7          | Same deal as partner play                                   |

First player out wins; on a block, the lowest pip count in a single hand wins.

## Table Rules

Agree these before the first pose — they vary house to house:

- Whether the opening pose must be the double six or the poser may "sport" any bone.
- Whether the key bone is worth the extra point.
- Whether a blocked game counts toward the match at all.
- Whether scores reset on an opponent's win, or simply accumulate to 6.

## Quick Tips

- Count the sixes and blanks first — hard ends are usually built on them.
- Pose to a number you hold several of, so you can answer whatever comes back.
- Your partner's pass is information for the whole table, so use it before your opponents do.
- Holding a double late is dangerous; it only ever plays on one number.
