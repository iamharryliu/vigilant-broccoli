# Potatoes

All cultivated potatoes descend from _Solanum tuberosum_, domesticated in the Andes (modern Peru and Bolivia) around 8000 BC and carried to Europe by the Spanish in the 1500s. "Origin" below means where the named variety was bred or first selected, not where potatoes come from.

## Starch Bands

Ordered high starch to low — the single property that predicts most behaviour in the pan.

| Band           | Starch  | Behaviour                                   | Examples                               |
| -------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Floury (mealy) | ~20–22% | Cells separate — fluffy, absorbent, crumbly | Russet, King Edward, Maris Piper       |
| All-purpose    | ~16–18% | Middle ground — creamy, mostly holds shape  | Yukon Gold, Desiree, White, Kennebec   |
| Waxy           | ~14–16% | Cells stay bound — firm, glossy, sliceable  | Red, Fingerling, Charlotte, New Potato |

## Russet vs Yukon Gold

| Trait          | Russet                                | Yukon Gold                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Origin         | USA, Luther Burbank line              | Canada, University of Guelph, 1980      |
| Starch / water | High starch, low moisture             | Medium starch, higher moisture          |
| Skin           | Thick, rough, usually peeled          | Thin, can be left on                    |
| Flesh          | White, dry                            | Yellow, waxy-creamy                     |
| Boiled         | Falls apart                           | Holds shape                             |
| Mashed         | Light and fluffy, drinks butter/cream | Dense and naturally buttery, less cream |
| Fried          | Crispest exterior, fluffy interior    | Crisps less, browns faster (more sugar) |
| Roasted        | Crunchy shell, soft inside            | Golden, creamy inside, firmer edges     |

## Buying and Storing

- Store cool, dark, and dry — not in the fridge, where cold converts starch to sugar and makes fries brown too fast and taste sweet.
- Keep away from onions; onions release gases that sprout potatoes faster.
- Green skin means solanine from light exposure — cut it away generously, or discard if the potato is green throughout.
- Sprouts alone are fine to rub off if the potato is still firm; a soft, wrinkled potato is spent.
