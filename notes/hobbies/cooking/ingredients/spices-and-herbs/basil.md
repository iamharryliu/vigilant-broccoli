## Varieties

| Variety | Flavour | Common Uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genovese (sweet) | Sweet, clove-like, slightly peppery | Pesto, caprese, pasta, pizza |
| Holy basil (tulsi) | Peppery, spicy, clove-like | Thai stir-fries such as pad kra pao |
| Lemon basil | Citrusy, light | Salads, fish, drinks |
| Purple (opal) basil | Mild, slightly spicy | Garnish, salads, infused vinegar |
| Thai basil | Anise and liquorice notes, holds up to heat | Curries, noodle soups, stir-fries |

## Buying

- Choose bright, glossy leaves with no black spots or wilting.
- A potted plant with roots lasts longer than cut bunches and can be harvested for weeks.

## Storing

- Never refrigerate loose leaves; cold turns basil black and limp.
- Keep cut stems in a glass of water at room temperature, out of direct sun, like flowers.
- Freeze as pesto or as leaves blended with olive oil in ice-cube trays.
- Dried basil keeps its flavour but loses the fresh, sweet aroma; use roughly a third of the fresh amount.

## Cooking

- Add at the end of cooking or raw; heat quickly dulls the aroma and darkens the leaves.
- Tear by hand or cut into ribbons (chiffonade) just before using, since cut edges bruise and blacken.
- Hardy Thai and holy basil can go into the pan; sweet basil should not.

## Pairings

- Tomato, mozzarella, olive oil, garlic, pine nuts and parmesan.
- Zucchini, eggplant, strawberries, peaches and lemon.
- Included in [Italian Seasoning](./italian-seasoning.md) when dried.
