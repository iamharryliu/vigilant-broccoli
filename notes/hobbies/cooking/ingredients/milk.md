## Dairy Milk

Fat content is what separates most cow's milk products from each other, and it decides how they behave with heat — the more fat, the less likely a sauce is to split when it simmers or meets acid. Cultured and concentrated milks are the same milk with water removed or bacteria added.

| Milk               | Fat    | Description                                                                          | Common Uses                                      |
| ------------------ | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Buttermilk         | ~1%    | Cultured and tangy; its acid tenderizes and reacts with baking soda for lift.        | Pancakes, biscuits, fried chicken brine, ranch   |
| Condensed Milk     | ~8%    | Evaporated milk with sugar added, thick and syrupy.                                  | Desserts, Vietnamese coffee, fudge, tres leches  |
| Evaporated Milk    | ~8%    | Milk reduced by about half, unsweetened and heat-stable.                             | Pumpkin pie, mac and cheese, canned-goods baking |
| Goat's Milk        | ~4%    | Tangier and more strongly flavored than cow's; smaller fat globules, no cream layer. | Drinking, chèvre, caramel sauces                 |
| Half-and-Half      | ~11%   | Equal parts milk and cream.                                                          | Coffee, light cream sauces                       |
| Heavy Cream        | ~36%   | Whips to stiff peaks and won't break when boiled or acidified.                       | Whipped cream, ganache, pan sauces, ice cream    |
| Raw Milk           | ~4%    | Unpasteurized and unhomogenized; cream separates and rises.                          | Drinking where legal, farmhouse cheesemaking     |
| Sheep's Milk       | ~7%    | Richest of the common dairy milks, high in protein and solids.                       | Feta, pecorino, roquefort, yogurt                |
| Skim (Nonfat) Milk | ~0%    | Fat removed; thin and prone to scorching or splitting in sauces.                     | Drinking, lightening coffee, low-fat baking      |
| Sour Cream         | ~20%   | Cultured cream, thick and tangy, splits if boiled hard.                              | Finishing soups, dips, baking, toppings          |
| Whole Milk         | ~3.25% | The default for cooking — enough fat to carry flavor and resist curdling.            | Drinking, béchamel, custards, baking             |
| Yogurt             | 0–10%  | Milk fermented to a set, tangy curd; strained for Greek-style thickness.             | Breakfast, marinades, raita, sauces              |

## Plant Milk

Plant milks are extractions, not emulsions of dairy fat, so they behave differently under heat — most curdle in hot coffee unless "barista" versions add stabilizers, and none brown or set like dairy.

| Milk         | Base          | Description                                                                      | Common Uses                                |
| ------------ | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Almond Milk  | Almonds       | Thin and nutty, low in protein; separates readily when heated.                   | Cereal, smoothies, drinking                |
| Cashew Milk  | Cashews       | Creamy with little separation, mild flavor.                                      | Cream sauces, soups, coffee                |
| Coconut Milk | Coconut flesh | High in saturated fat, thick and stable at a simmer; canned is the cooking form. | Curries, braises, desserts, ice cream      |
| Oat Milk     | Oats          | Naturally sweet and creamy, steams and foams well.                               | Coffee drinks, baking, cereal              |
| Rice Milk    | Rice          | Thinnest and sweetest, watery in sauces and lowest in protein.                   | Drinking, allergen-free substitutions      |
| Soy Milk     | Soybeans      | Closest to dairy in protein, so it emulsifies and curdles like milk.             | Coffee, baking, tofu-making, savory dishes |
