# Restaurant Types

| Type        | Description                                                                                                |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Bistro      | Small, casual European-style restaurant with a limited, home-style menu and simple decor.                    |
| Buffet      | Self-serve restaurant with dishes laid out for diners to serve themselves, usually for a flat price.          |
| Cafe        | Casual spot focused on coffee, light bites, and pastries, often with all-day seating.                        |
| Fast Casual | Counter-service ordering like fast food, but with higher-quality ingredients and no tableside service.        |
| Fast Food   | Quick-service restaurant built around speed and a standardized, inexpensive menu.                             |
| Fine Dining | Upscale restaurant with formal service, curated tasting or à la carte menus, and a premium atmosphere.       |
| Food Truck  | Mobile kitchen serving a focused menu from a truck or cart, typically at a fixed spot or rotating locations.   |
| Gastropub   | Pub that puts the same care into its food menu as its drinks, blending bar culture with chef-driven cooking. |
| Izakaya     | Japanese casual bar serving small, shareable plates meant to be paired with drinks over a long, relaxed meal. |
| Omakase     | Chef-driven tasting menu, most associated with sushi, where the chef chooses each course for the diner.       |
| Pop-up      | Temporary restaurant or dining event, often run by a chef without a permanent location, open for a limited run. |
| Steakhouse  | Restaurant centered on grilled or broiled steaks and other cuts of meat, usually with a hearty, classic menu. |
| Tapas Bar   | Spanish-style bar serving small shared plates meant to be ordered in variety across a table.                  |
