# Cooking Nuances

| Nuance                                 | Difference                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **White miso vs dark miso**            | White miso is mildly sweet, lightly fermented, and delicate. Dark miso is stronger, saltier, and aged much longer for deeper umami.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Light soy sauce vs dark soy sauce**  | Light soy sauce is thinner, saltier, and primarily used for seasoning. Dark soy sauce is thicker, slightly sweeter, less salty, and mainly used to add rich color and depth.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Mirin vs rice wine (sake)**          | Mirin is a sweet Japanese cooking wine with a high sugar content that adds sweetness and gloss to dishes. Rice wine (sake) is much drier and less sweet, contributing subtle flavor without adding significant sweetness.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Unbleached flour vs bleached flour** | Unbleached flour whitens naturally as it ages, keeping more protein strength and a slight off-white tint — better for bread, puff pastry, and anything needing structure. Bleached flour is chemically treated to whiten and weaken the gluten faster, giving softer, more tender crumb in cakes, biscuits, and cookies.                                                                                                  |
| **Butter vs clarified butter**         | Butter is ~80% fat with water and milk solids, so it browns and burns at a low smoke point (~150°C/300°F) but carries a creamy, milky flavor — good for baking, sauces, and finishing. Clarified butter (ghee when cooked further) has the water and milk solids removed, giving nearly pure fat with a high smoke point (~250°C/480°F), a nuttier taste, and a long shelf life — good for searing, frying, and sautéing. |
