## Knife Anatomy

<img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Kitchen_Knife_Anatomy.png/500px-Kitchen_Knife_Anatomy.png" width="500" alt="Labelled diagram of a chef's knife">

| Label | Part         | Description                                                                                                                         |
| ----- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A     | Point        | The very end of the knife, used for piercing                                                                                        |
| B     | Tip          | Roughly the first third of the blade, used for small or delicate work — called the belly or curve when curved, as on a chef's knife |
| C     | Edge         | The entire cutting surface, from point to heel; bevelled or symmetric                                                               |
| D     | Heel         | The rear of the blade, for cuts that need more force                                                                                |
| E     | Spine        | The top, thicker portion of the blade — adds weight and strength                                                                    |
| F     | Bolster      | The thick metal join between handle and blade — adds weight and balance                                                             |
| G     | Finger Guard | The part of the bolster that stops your hand slipping onto the blade                                                                |
| H     | Choil        | Where the heel meets the bolster                                                                                                    |
| J     | Tang         | The blade metal extending into the handle, giving stability and weight                                                              |
| K     | Scales       | The two pieces of handle material attached to either side of the tang                                                               |
| L     | Rivets       | The metal pins (usually three) holding the scales to the tang                                                                       |
| M     | Handle Guard | The lip below the butt, for grip and to prevent slipping                                                                            |
| N     | Butt         | The terminal end of the handle                                                                                                      |

## Knife Material

| Material        | Edge                                                   | Maintenance                                                                    | Best For                                    |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| Stainless Steel | Harder to get as sharp, dulls faster than carbon steel | Resists rust and staining, low maintenance                                     | Good default for most home kitchens         |
| Carbon Steel    | Takes and holds a sharper edge, easier to resharpen    | Reacts with acidic foods and rusts if left wet — dry immediately after washing | Many professional and Japanese-style knives |

## Western vs Japanese

| Style                        | Steel & Blade                       | Edge Angle       | Best For                                                                |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Western (e.g. German knives) | Softer steel, thicker/heavier blade | ~20° per side    | More forgiving, rocking cuts and heavier tasks (bones, joints)          |
| Japanese                     | Harder steel, thinner/lighter blade | ~10-15° per side | Sharper and more precise, more brittle — avoid twisting or cutting bone |

## Knife Management

- Routine: hone regularly during/between use, sharpen with a whetstone only when the knife stays dull after honing.

### Care & Storage

- Hand wash and dry immediately — dishwashers dull and corrode edges.
- Store on a magnetic strip, in a block, or with blade guards; loose in a drawer chips the edge.
- Cut on wood or plastic — glass, stone, and ceramic boards ruin an edge fast.
