## Disciplines

| Term                                              | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Inline Skates / Rollerblades](./inline-skate.md) | Skates with wheels arranged in a single line. Commonly called "Rollerblades" after Rollerblade, the first company to produce and popularize them — "roller" for the wheels, "blade" for the single line, like an ice skate blade. |
| [Roller Skates / Quad Skates](./rollerskate.md)   | Skates with four wheels in a rectangular pattern, two at the front and two at the back.                                                                                                                                           |
| [Skateboard](./skateboard.md)                     | Deck on two trucks and four wheels, ridden standing across the board rather than strapped or laced in.                                                                                                                            |
| [Snowboard](./snowboard.md)                       | Single board bound to both feet for snow — a related board discipline rather than a skate.                                                                                                                                        |

## Wheels

Every skate wheel — inline, quad, or skateboard — is urethane cast around a hub, described the same way: diameter in millimetres, hardness in durometer, plus a profile.

### Diameter

Measured in millimetres across the wheel.

| Bigger wheel                            | Smaller wheel                     |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Higher top speed, keeps roll longer     | Quicker acceleration and stopping |
| Rolls over cracks, gravel, and seams    | Catches on rough ground           |
| Raises ride height, less stable at rest | Lower centre of gravity           |
| Heavier, slower to swing around         | Lighter, easier for tricks        |

Typical ranges:

| Skate type           | Diameter  |
| -------------------- | --------- |
| Inline (aggressive)  | 55–64mm   |
| Inline (speed)       | 100–125mm |
| Inline (urban)       | 76–110mm  |
| Quad (derby, rink)   | 56–62mm   |
| Quad (outdoor)       | 62–70mm   |
| Skateboard (cruiser) | 55–65mm   |
| Skateboard (street)  | 50–55mm   |

### Durometer

Hardness, almost always on the Shore **A** scale (0–100A) — higher is harder. Very hard skateboard wheels use the **B** scale, which reads roughly 20 points lower than A, so 84B ≈ 104A.

Softer urethane deforms into the ground for grip and vibration damping, but wears faster and drags on smooth floors.

| Durometer | Feel                                | Use                                          |
| --------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 74A–80A   | Very soft, grippy, absorbs cracks   | Outdoor quads, cruiser boards, rough asphalt |
| 81A–87A   | Soft, still grippy but rolls better | Outdoor inline, all-round cruising           |
| 88A–95A   | Medium, mild slide                  | Urban inline, hybrid rink/outdoor quads      |
| 96A–101A  | Hard, slides easily, buzzy          | Street skateboarding, smooth rink quads      |
| 84B+      | Hardest, minimal grip               | Park and street skateboarding, derby sprints |

Rougher ground and heavier skaters both push the choice softer; polished floors and slide tricks push it harder.

### Profile

The shape of the contact patch. A rounded or bullet profile touches less ground, so it rolls easier and leans into turns; a flat, wide profile puts more urethane down for grip and stability.

Dual-durometer wheels pair a harder core with a softer outer layer to get grip without losing roll.

### Wear

Wheels wear unevenly — inside edges on inline and quad skates, one side on a skateboard. Rotate them on a schedule, and replace once coned, flat-spotted, or worn to the hub.

### Ramp Anatomy

| Term                | Description                                                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Coping              | Metal or concrete edge pipe along the lip, used for grinds, slides, and stalls.        |
| Deck                | Flat platform on top of a ramp where skaters stand and drop in from.                   |
| Flat Bottom         | Flat ground between two transitions, where speed is pumped and tricks are set up.      |
| Lip                 | The top edge of a ramp, bowl, or pool, where the transition ends and the coping sits.  |
| Roll-In             | Sloped entry onto a feature, ridden into rather than dropped in.                       |
| Transition (Tranny) | The curved section between flat ground and vertical; its radius sets how a ramp rides. |
| Vert                | The truly vertical top section of a transition, just below the coping.                 |

## City Skate Planning

### Preparation

- Post an outline of the line that you are planning to skate.
- Have support from other strong skaters to keep the group safe.

### During Skate

- Share a live location.
- Have first aid kits, skate tools, and spare skate parts.

### Lessons from Preview Skates

#### Food Skate

- Share food map with group before the skate starts.
- 1 hour of time at designated food spots.
- Weekdays may be better than weekends.
