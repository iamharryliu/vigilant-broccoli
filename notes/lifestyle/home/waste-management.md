# Waste Management

| Item                                     | How to dispose                                                                                                       | Why it is confusing                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Aerosol cans                             | Empty fully, then recycle with metal. Partly full cans go to household hazardous waste.                              | Pressurized and flammable contents, but the empty metal is recyclable.      |
| Batteries (alkaline, AA/AAA/9V)          | Drop-off collection at hardware or electronics stores; some areas allow trash. Tape 9V terminals.                    | Rules differ by region; looks harmless but can leak.                        |
| Batteries (lithium-ion, lithium, button) | Never in trash or recycling. Tape terminals and use a store or household hazardous waste drop-off.                   | Fire risk in trucks and facilities.                                         |
| Broken glass and mirrors                 | Wrap in paper or cardboard, label, and put in trash.                                                                 | Not recyclable with bottles and jars; different melting point and coatings. |
| Ceramics, dishes and drinking glasses    | Wrap and put in trash, or donate if intact.                                                                          | Contaminates glass recycling.                                               |
| Coffee pods                              | Peel foil and remove grounds (compost), then recycle the plastic cup if accepted. Otherwise use a take-back program. | Mixed materials; many are too small to sort.                                |
| Cooking oil and grease                   | Cool, pour into a sealed container and put in trash, or use a drop-off for used cooking oil. Never down the drain.   | Clogs pipes and sewers.                                                     |
| Electronics                              | E-waste drop-off or manufacturer/retailer take-back. Wipe personal data first.                                       | Contain toxic metals and reusable materials; often banned from landfill.    |
| Food-soiled paper and pizza boxes        | Compost if accepted. Clean top lid can be recycled; greasy bottom cannot.                                            | Grease contaminates paper recycling.                                        |
| Light bulbs (CFL and fluorescent tubes)  | Hazardous waste or retailer drop-off.                                                                                | Contain mercury.                                                            |
| Light bulbs (LED and incandescent)       | Trash, or e-waste drop-off for LED where offered.                                                                    | Not recyclable with glass.                                                  |
| Lighters                                 | Use up fuel and put in trash. Full or partly full go to household hazardous waste.                                   | Flammable fuel in a small plastic body.                                     |
| Medications                              | Pharmacy take-back box or collection event. Do not flush unless the label says so.                                   | Contaminates water; can be misused.                                         |
| Needles and sharps                       | Sealed puncture-proof sharps container, then pharmacy or health-unit drop-off.                                       | Injury risk to handlers.                                                    |
| Paint                                    | Dry out latex with cat litter and trash it (lid off), or use a paint drop-off. Oil-based goes to hazardous waste.    | Liquid paint is not accepted in trash or recycling.                         |
| Pet waste                                | Bag and put in trash. Do not compost.                                                                                | Pathogens; plastic bags are not compostable unless certified.               |
| Plastic bags and film                    | Store drop-off bins at grocery stores, not curbside recycling. Reuse where possible.                                 | Jams sorting machinery.                                                     |
| Propane and fuel canisters               | Return to the retailer or hazardous waste drop-off. Never trash or recycle.                                          | Explosion risk.                                                             |
| Razor blades and other loose sharps      | Put in a sealed rigid container (e.g. a metal can or sharps bin), label, then trash.                                 | Cut hazard for handlers; too small to recycle.                              |
| Receipts (thermal paper)                 | Trash.                                                                                                               | Coating contaminates paper recycling.                                       |
| Styrofoam (expanded polystyrene)         | Clean foam packaging to a dedicated drop-off if available; otherwise trash. Food containers go to trash.             | Rarely accepted curbside; crumbles into litter.                             |
| Used motor oil and filters               | Seal in a container and take to an auto shop or hazardous waste depot. Never drain or trash.                         | One of the worst water pollutants but highly recyclable.                    |
