# Textile Patterns

Common fabric pattern/print names, what defines each, and a reference image.

| Pattern             | Image                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Description                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Argyle**          | ![Argyle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/PinkArgyle.svg/250px-PinkArgyle.svg.png)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Diamonds in alternating colors, overlaid with a diagonal crossing line (intercrossing diagonal stripes), historically tied to Scottish tartan and knitwear. |
| **Batik**           | ![Batik](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5f/Batik_Trusmi_Cirebon_%2823%29.jpg/250px-Batik_Trusmi_Cirebon_%2823%29.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Wax-resist dyeing technique (not a fixed motif) producing intricate patterns, traditional to Indonesia/Java.                                                |
| **Chevron**         | ![Chevron](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Textile_Design_Met_DP889374.jpg/250px-Textile_Design_Met_DP889374.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Repeating V-shaped zigzag stripes; distinct from herringbone in that the stripes meet at a point rather than offsetting.                                    |
| **Damask**          | ![Damask](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1f/ItalianSilkDamask.jpg/250px-ItalianSilkDamask.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Reversible woven pattern (not printed) with a shiny/matte contrast in the same color, usually floral or ornamental; named for Damascus.                     |
| **Gingham**         | ![Gingham](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Greengingham.jpg/250px-Greengingham.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Even, small-scale checks formed by woven stripes crossing perpendicular in two colors (usually with white).                                                 |
| **Herringbone**     | ![Herringbone](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6c/90_degree_Herringbone_bond.png/250px-90_degree_Herringbone_bond.png)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Broken zigzag of offset parallel lines resembling fish bones; a twill weave variant, common in tweed.                                                       |
| **Houndstooth**     | ![Houndstooth](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/Houndstooth.svg/250px-Houndstooth.svg.png)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Broken/jagged two-tone check with a pointed, abstract four-pointed shape, traditional to Scottish wool fabric.                                              |
| **Ikat**            | ![Ikat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Indonesian_funeral_shroud_or_hanging%2C_%28porilonjong%29%2C_Central_Sulawesi_%28Celebes%29%2C_Rongkong%2C_Toraja%2C_cotton_with_ikat_paterns.jpg/250px-Indonesian_funeral_shroud_or_hanging%2C_%28porilonjong%29%2C_Central_Sulawesi_%28Celebes%29%2C_Rongkong%2C_Toraja%2C_cotton_with_ikat_paterns.jpg) | Resist-dyeing technique applied to the yarn before weaving, giving motifs a characteristic blurred/feathered edge.                                          |
| **Madras**          | ![Madras](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Patchwork_madras_fabric%2C_made_from_Indian_cotton_madras_plaid.jpg/250px-Patchwork_madras_fabric%2C_made_from_Indian_cotton_madras_plaid.jpg)                                                                                                                                                           | Lightweight cotton plaid in irregular, brightly colored checks, originating from Madras (Chennai), India.                                                   |
| **Paisley**         | ![Paisley](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Shawl_LACMA_M.84.244.1.jpg/250px-Shawl_LACMA_M.84.244.1.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Teardrop/curved-boteh motif originating from Persian and Indian textiles, popularized in the West via shawls made in Paisley, Scotland.                     |
| **Pinstripe**       | ![Pinstripe](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Pinstripe.jpg/250px-Pinstripe.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Very thin, evenly spaced vertical stripes, classically on suiting fabric.                                                                                   |
| **Polka Dot**       | ![Polka Dot](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Polka_dots.svg/250px-Polka_dots.svg.png)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Regularly spaced, equally sized filled circles on a contrasting background.                                                                                 |
| **Tartan / Plaid**  | ![Tartan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0d/Three_tartans.jpg/250px-Three_tartans.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Criss-crossed horizontal and vertical bands of multiple colors forming repeating rectangular blocks; specific tartans are tied to Scottish clans.           |
| **Toile (de Jouy)** | ![Toile](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/Handkerchief.jpg/250px-Handkerchief.jpg)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Single-color detailed pastoral/scenic illustrations printed repeatedly on an off-white background, originating in Jouy-en-Josas, France.                    |
