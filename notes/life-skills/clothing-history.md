# Clothing History

Where everyday garments came from, and how each moved from its original purpose into general wear.

| Garment           | Image                                                                                                                                       | Origin                                                                                 | History                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Bomber Jacket** | <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/df/MA-1_Jacket_in_petrol.jpg" width="160" />                                     | Military aviation, WWI-era Europe and the US Army Air Service (1910s–20s)              | Early cockpits were open and unheated, so pilots were issued heavy leather flight jackets with fur collars and snug cuffs and waistbands to seal warmth in. Successive issue patterns refined it — leather A-1 and A-2, shearling B-3, then the nylon MA-1 of the 1950s once pressurised cockpits made bulk unnecessary. The MA-1's short, elastic-hemmed shape is what most "bomber" jackets still copy, having been picked up postwar as surplus by mods, skinheads, punks, and hip-hop, and eventually by mainstream fashion. |
| **Chelsea Boots** | <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Chelsea_boot%2C_black.jpg" width="160" />                                     | Victorian London, 1830s–40s, credited to Queen Victoria's bootmaker J. Sparkes-Hall    | Built around newly available vulcanised rubber: an elastic gusset on each side let a close-fitting riding boot be pulled on and off without laces or buttons, with a loop at the heel for leverage. The ankle-height, seam-light shape stayed a riding and walking boot for a century, then was picked up by 1950s–60s King's Road London — the Beatles had theirs made with a Cuban heel, and the style has been read as mod ever since.                                                                                        |
| **Cowboy Boots**  | <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Cowboy_boots_cropped.png/330px-Cowboy_boots_cropped.png" width="160" /> | American West cattle drives, 1860s–70s, adapted from Spanish vaquero and cavalry boots | Built for working from horseback: a tall shaft to protect the leg against brush and snakes, a pointed toe to find the stirrup quickly, and an underslung heel high enough that the foot cannot slide through it. Bootmakers in Kansas and Texas shaped the form as drovers ordered variations, and decorative stitching — originally functional, keeping the tall leather shaft upright — grew ornate. Hollywood Westerns and later country music turned the boot into a style item worn far from any horse.                     |
| **Jeans**         | <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/Jeans.jpg" width="160" />                                                     | Patented 1873 by Jacob Davis and Levi Strauss & Co. for American labourers             | Davis, a Reno tailor, had been reinforcing work trousers with copper rivets at the pockets and fly base — the points that tore first on miners and teamsters — and partnered with his denim supplier Levi Strauss to patent it. They stayed workwear until mid-century, when Westerns, Marlon Brando, and James Dean made them a symbol of youth rebellion, and school bans in the 1950s only cemented that.                                                                                                                     |
| **Trench Coat**   | <img src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/61/WWITrenchcoat.jpeg" width="160" />                                            | British officers in WWI, developed from earlier Burberry and Aquascutum rainwear       | Made from tightly woven waterproof gabardine so officers could stay dry without the weight of a rubberised greatcoat. Nearly every detail was equipment: the storm flap shed rain off the shoulder, epaulettes held rank insignia and gloves, the throat latch sealed the collar, and the D-ring belt carried map cases and sidearms. Officers bought them privately and kept wearing them home, and postwar film noir and cinema detectives fixed the coat as civilian outerwear.                                               |
