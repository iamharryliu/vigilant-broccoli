## Types of Words

| Type | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Adjective** | Describes or modifies nouns. | beautiful, tall, quick |
| **Adverb** | Modifies verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs. | quickly, very, silently |
| **Conjunction** | Connects words, phrases, or clauses. | and, but, because, although |
| **Determiner (Article)** | Introduces a noun and says which one or how many. | the, a, this, some, my |
| **Interjection** | Expresses sudden emotion or reaction, standing apart from the sentence grammar. | wow, ouch, hey |
| **Noun** | Represents people, places, things, or ideas. | dog, house, freedom, Alex |
| **Preposition** | Used before a noun, pronoun, or noun phrase to show direction, time, place, location, spatial relationships, or to introduce an object. | in, on, at, by, with, of, to |
| **Pronoun** | Stands in for a noun to avoid repetition. | he, she, it, they, this, who |
| **Verb** | Indicates actions, states, or occurrences. | run, think, is, become |

## Verb Forms and Tenses

| Form | Purpose | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Infinitive** | Base form of the verb, often "to + verb." | To run, to eat, to sleep |
| **Imperative** | Commands or instructions. | Run! Eat! Please, sleep. |
| **Present** | Describes current or habitual actions. | I run, she eats, they sleep. |
| **Imperfect** | Describes ongoing or repeated actions in the past. | I was running, she used to eat, they slept. |
| **Past (Simple)** | Describes completed actions in the past. | I ran, she ate, they slept. |
| **Future** | Describes actions that will happen. | I will run, she will eat, they will sleep. |
| **Perfect** | Describes completed actions (often with relevance to now). | I have run, she has eaten, they have slept. |
| **Pluperfect (Past Perfect)** | Describes actions completed before another past action. | I had run, she had eaten, they had slept. |
| **Conditional** | Describes actions dependent on conditions. | I would run, she would eat, they would sleep. |
| **Subjunctive** | Expresses doubt, wishes, or hypothetical situations. | If I were running, she might eat, they should sleep. |

## Terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Antonym** | A word with the opposite meaning of another; found across adjectives, nouns, verbs, adverbs and prepositions (e.g., hot/cold, buy/sell, in/out). Gradable pairs have a middle ground (hot/cold), complementary pairs do not (alive/dead), and relational pairs imply each other (buy/sell). |
| **Auxiliary/Helper Verb** | A helper verb that carries grammar (questions, tense, negation, mood, voice), while the main verb carries meaning (e.g., it _is_ raining). |
| **Definite Form** | Refers to a specific noun that is known to both the speaker and the listener (e.g., the dog, the house). |
| **Indefinite Form** | Refers to a non-specific noun or one that is being mentioned for the first time (e.g., a dog, an apple). |
| **Inversion** | A change in the normal word order of a sentence — most commonly when the verb is placed before the subject instead of after it. Normally used to indicate a question. |
| **Object** | The noun or pronoun that receives the action of the verb in a sentence. |
| **Objective Pronoun** | A pronoun used as the object of a verb or preposition (e.g., me, you, him, her, it, us, them). |
| **Personal Pronoun** | A pronoun that refers to a specific person or thing (e.g., I, you, he, she, it, we, they). |
| **Plural** | Refers to more than one person, thing, or idea (e.g., cats, children). |
| **Possessive Pronoun** | A pronoun that shows ownership and stands alone in place of a noun (e.g., mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs). |
| **Reflexive Pronoun** | A form of a pronoun that refers back to the subject of the sentence (e.g., myself, yourself, himself). |
| **Relative Pronoun** | A pronoun that introduces a clause giving more information about a noun (e.g., who, whom, whose, which, that). |
| **Singular** | Refers to one person, thing, or idea (e.g., cat, child). |
| **Subject** | The noun or pronoun that performs the action of the verb in a sentence. |
