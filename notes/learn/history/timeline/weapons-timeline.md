# Weapons Timeline

| Category              | How It Works                                                                                                                                   | Pros                                                                                         | Cons                                                                                    | Region of Origin       | Approx. Date |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| Stone Tools           | Sharpened or flaked stone (flint, obsidian) shaped into a cutting or piercing edge, held or hafted onto a handle.                              | Abundant raw material; simple to produce.                                                    | Brittle, dulls quickly, limited range and lethality.                                    | Africa                 | ~600,000 BCE |
| Spears & Arrows       | A pointed head (stone/bone) mounted on a shaft, thrown by hand or launched from a bow to strike at range.                                      | Extended reach beyond melee; arrows let one bow be reused for many shots.                    | Limited penetration against armor; requires practice to aim accurately.                 | Africa/Asia            | ~100,000 BCE |
| Bronze Weapons        | Copper and tin alloyed and cast into blades and points, harder and more durable than stone.                                                    | Durable, resharpenable edge; castable into complex shapes.                                   | Tin was scarce and costly; still softer than iron or steel.                             | Mesopotamia            | ~3,300 BCE   |
| Iron Weapons          | Iron ore smelted and forged into blades, later heat-treated for a harder edge than bronze.                                                     | Iron ore far more abundant than tin/copper; harder, stronger edge.                           | Prone to rust; required skilled smithing infrastructure.                                | Anatolia/Mediterranean | ~1,200 BCE   |
| Cavalry & Chariots    | Horses carry riders or pull wheeled platforms into battle, giving a speed and mobility advantage.                                              | Speed, mobility, shock value against infantry.                                               | Costly to breed, train, and maintain; vulnerable on rough terrain.                      | Mesopotamia/Eurasia    | ~2,000 BCE   |
| Classical Infantry    | Coordinated formations of foot soldiers (e.g. the Greek phalanx, Roman legion) fighting with shields, spears, and swords in disciplined ranks. | Discipline and formation multiply individual effectiveness.                                  | Requires extensive training and logistics; vulnerable to flanking or broken terrain.    | Greece/Rome            | ~700 BCE     |
| Crossbows             | A mechanical bow holds a bolt under tension via a trigger mechanism, releasing high force with less user strength or training.                 | Easier to train users than longbows; high penetrating power.                                 | Slower reload rate than bows; heavier and bulkier.                                      | China                  | ~500 BCE     |
| Gunpowder Weapons     | Ignited gunpowder rapidly expands into gas, propelling a projectile out of a tube or barrel.                                                   | High lethality regardless of user strength; effective against armor.                         | Early gunpowder was unreliable and slow to reload; required a chemical supply chain.    | China                  | ~900 CE      |
| Cannons & Artillery   | Large-caliber gunpowder weapons fire heavy projectiles over long distances, often to breach walls or fortifications.                           | Devastating against fortifications and massed troops.                                        | Heavy, immobile, slow to reload and reposition.                                         | China → Middle East    | ~1200 CE     |
| Muskets & Rifles      | A shoulder-fired gunpowder weapon sends a single projectile through a barrel; rifling later spins the bullet for accuracy.                     | Standard-issue infantry firepower; rifling greatly improved range and accuracy.              | Slow reload (especially muzzle-loaders); muskets were inaccurate at range.              | Eurasia                | ~1400 CE     |
| Pistols & Revolvers   | Compact single-hand firearms; a revolver's rotating cylinder holds multiple rounds ready to fire without reloading each shot.                  | Portable; revolvers allow rapid multiple shots without reloading.                            | Shorter range and lower stopping power than long guns.                                  | Europe/USA             | ~1600 CE     |
| Machine Guns          | An automatic mechanism cycles loading, firing, and ejecting using recoil or gas pressure, sustaining continuous fire.                          | Extremely high rate of sustained fire; devastating against massed infantry.                  | Heavy, needs a steady ammunition supply and often a crew; prone to overheating.         | Europe/USA             | ~1800s CE    |
| Armored Vehicles      | Engine-powered vehicles protected by metal armor plating, often armed with cannons or machine guns, cross terrain under fire.                  | Mobile protected firepower; can breach fortified or defended lines.                          | Expensive, fuel-dependent, vulnerable to anti-tank weapons and mines.                   | Europe                 | ~1900s CE    |
| Air Power (Planes)    | Powered heavier-than-air aircraft deliver bombs, missiles, or gunfire from above, beyond the reach of ground defenses.                         | Extends reach far beyond ground forces; enables strategic bombing and reconnaissance.        | Costly to build and maintain; vulnerable to anti-air defenses and weather.              | Global                 | ~1900s CE    |
| Nuclear Weapons       | Nuclear fission or fusion releases immense energy in a single explosion, far beyond any chemical explosive.                                    | Unmatched destructive power; strong deterrent effect.                                        | Catastrophic and indiscriminate; long-term radioactive fallout.                         | USA                    | 1945 CE      |
| Precision Missiles    | Self-propelled guided munitions use onboard guidance (radar, GPS, inertial) to steer toward a specific target over long range.                 | High accuracy at long range; reduces the need to expose personnel.                           | Expensive per unit; dependent on complex guidance and targeting systems.                | USA/USSR               | ~1960 CE     |
| Digital/Drone Warfare | Remotely piloted or autonomous unmanned vehicles, plus cyber operations, extend reach and reduce risk to operators.                            | Removes operators from direct danger; enables persistent surveillance and strike capability. | Vulnerable to jamming or hacking; raises accountability and civilian-casualty concerns. | Global                 | ~2000 CE     |
