# Vehicle Power Timeline

| Power Source                 | Approx. Date | Region of Origin | Notes                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Horses & Draft Animals         | ~3,500 BCE   | Eurasian Steppe    | Domesticated horses pulling carts/chariots predate any mechanical vehicle by millennia.          |
| Sailing Ships                 | ~3,000 BCE   | Egypt/Mesopotamia | Wind power for water transport, running alongside animal power on land for most of history.       |
| Steam (Coal-Fired)             | 1769 CE      | France/UK          | Cugnot's steam tricycle, then rail locomotives; external combustion burning coal to boil water.  |
| Internal Combustion (Gas)      | 1859 CE      | France             | Lenoir's gas engine ran on illuminating (coal) gas rather than liquid fuel.                       |
| Internal Combustion (Petrol)   | 1885 CE      | Germany            | Benz Patent-Motorwagen; the petrol/gasoline engine that became the automotive default.            |
| Diesel                        | 1897 CE      | Germany            | Rudolf Diesel's compression-ignition engine, adopted for trucks, rail, and marine use.            |
| Electric (Early)               | 1900s CE     | USA/Europe         | Battery electric cars were common alongside steam and petrol before petrol's range/refueling won out. |
| Wood Gas (Gasifier)            | 1900s CE     | Europe             | Vehicles retrofitted with gasifiers burning wood/charcoal to produce syngas; widespread during WWII fuel shortages when petrol was rationed. |
| Leaded Gasoline                | 1921 CE      | USA                | Tetraethyl lead added as an anti-knock agent; phased out from the 1970s-1990s over toxicity, banned for on-road use in the US by 1996 and globally by 2021. |
| CNG (Compressed Natural Gas)   | 1930s CE     | Italy              | Natural gas compressed for onboard storage, popular for fleets and buses.                          |
| Biogas (Anaerobic Digestion)   | 1930s CE     | Europe             | Methane from digested organic/agricultural waste used to run farm and municipal vehicles; still used today in compressed form (bio-CNG). |
| LPG (Autogas)                  | 1940s CE     | USA                | Liquefied petroleum gas as a cheaper, lower-emission alternative fuel for conversions.             |
| Hybrid (Gas-Electric)          | 1997 CE      | Japan              | Toyota Prius; production combustion-electric hybrid combining an engine with a battery/motor.     |
| Modern Battery Electric        | 2008 CE      | USA                | Tesla Roadster; lithium-ion battery electric vehicles reach mainstream range and performance.       |
| Hydrogen Fuel Cell            | 2014 CE      | Japan              | Toyota Mirai; hydrogen converted to electricity onboard, emitting only water.                       |
