# Vehicle Power Timeline

| Power Source                 | How It Works                                                                                                                                                | Pros                                                                                    | Cons                                                                                                        | Region of Origin  | Approx. Date |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------ |
| Horses & Draft Animals       | Domesticated horses pulling carts/chariots predate any mechanical vehicle by millennia.                                                                     | Renewable (self-reproducing); fuel is grass/feed, not a manufactured commodity.         | Limited speed and range; requires ongoing care and feeding; produces waste.                                 | Eurasian Steppe   | ~3,500 BCE   |
| Sailing Ships                | Wind power for water transport, running alongside animal power on land for most of history.                                                                 | Free fuel (wind); capable of very long range.                                           | Dependent on weather and wind direction; slow and unpredictable.                                            | Egypt/Mesopotamia | ~3,000 BCE   |
| Steam (Coal-Fired)           | Cugnot's steam tricycle, then rail locomotives; external combustion burning coal to boil water.                                                             | Powerful; ran on abundant, cheap coal.                                                  | Heavy, bulky boilers; slow to start while building pressure; inefficient.                                   | France/UK         | 1769 CE      |
| Internal Combustion (Gas)    | Lenoir's gas engine ran on illuminating (coal) gas rather than liquid fuel.                                                                                 | More compact than a steam engine; no external boiler needed.                            | Low power output; piped gas fuel was impractical for a mobile vehicle.                                      | France            | 1859 CE      |
| Internal Combustion (Petrol) | Benz Patent-Motorwagen; the petrol/gasoline engine that became the automotive default.                                                                      | High energy density; quick refueling; strong power-to-weight ratio.                     | Non-renewable fuel; air pollution; mechanically complex.                                                    | Germany           | 1885 CE      |
| Diesel                       | Rudolf Diesel's compression-ignition engine, adopted for trucks, rail, and marine use.                                                                      | More fuel-efficient and torquey than petrol; well suited to heavy loads.                | Noisier; historically dirtier emissions (particulates, NOx).                                                | Germany           | 1897 CE      |
| Electric (Early)             | Battery electric cars were common alongside steam and petrol before petrol's range/refueling won out.                                                       | Quiet, no tailpipe emissions, simple to drive (no gear shifting).                       | Limited range and battery capacity; long recharge time.                                                     | USA/Europe        | 1900s CE     |
| Wood Gas (Gasifier)          | Vehicles retrofitted with gasifiers burning wood/charcoal to produce syngas; widespread during WWII fuel shortages when petrol was rationed.                | Used cheap, widely available fuel (wood/charcoal) when petrol was scarce.               | Bulky equipment; reduced engine power; slow startup; produced tar and soot needing frequent cleaning.       | Europe            | 1900s CE     |
| Leaded Gasoline              | Tetraethyl lead added as an anti-knock agent; phased out from the 1970s-1990s over toxicity, banned for on-road use in the US by 1996 and globally by 2021. | Reduced engine knock; improved performance and efficiency for the era's engines.        | Highly toxic lead emissions; lasting environmental and public health damage.                                | USA               | 1921 CE      |
| CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) | Natural gas compressed for onboard storage, popular for fleets and buses.                                                                                   | Cleaner burning than gasoline; cheaper fuel cost.                                       | Bulky high-pressure tanks reduce range and cargo space; limited refueling infrastructure.                   | Italy             | 1930s CE     |
| Biogas (Anaerobic Digestion) | Methane from digested organic/agricultural waste used to run farm and municipal vehicles; still used today in compressed form (bio-CNG).                    | Renewable; made from waste; reduces methane that would otherwise escape untreated.      | Lower energy density; requires specialized storage; limited availability.                                   | Europe            | 1930s CE     |
| LPG (Autogas)                | Liquefied petroleum gas as a cheaper, lower-emission alternative fuel for conversions.                                                                      | Cheaper than gasoline; lower emissions; widely available in some regions.               | Lower energy density (reduced range); separate tank takes up cargo/trunk space.                             | USA               | 1940s CE     |
| Hybrid (Gas-Electric)        | Toyota Prius; production combustion-electric hybrid combining an engine with a battery/motor.                                                               | Better fuel economy than pure combustion; no range anxiety thanks to the backup engine. | More complex and costly than a conventional engine; still relies on fossil fuel.                            | Japan             | 1997 CE      |
| Modern Battery Electric      | Tesla Roadster; lithium-ion battery electric vehicles reach mainstream range and performance.                                                               | Zero tailpipe emissions; low running/maintenance costs; strong instant torque.          | Long charging times versus refueling; range limitations; battery production has its own environmental cost. | USA               | 2008 CE      |
| Hydrogen Fuel Cell           | Toyota Mirai; hydrogen converted to electricity onboard, emitting only water.                                                                               | Only emits water; refuels faster than a battery EV recharges.                           | Sparse refueling infrastructure; hydrogen production and storage are costly and inefficient.                | Japan             | 2014 CE      |
